# Growth Experimentation

## Overview

Growth experimentation is the systematic process of rapidly testing hypotheses across the entire customer journey to identify scalable, sustainable growth levers. Unlike traditional CRO which focuses primarily on conversion optimization, growth experimentation spans acquisition, activation, retention, revenue, and referral (AARRR framework), using a high-velocity testing approach to discover non-obvious growth opportunities.

Growth experimentation excels at:
- **Rapid Iteration**: High-velocity testing across all growth levers
- **Cross-Functional Growth**: Experiments spanning product, marketing, sales
- **Systematic Discovery**: Finding non-obvious growth opportunities
- **Metric-Driven**: Focus on North Star metrics and growth levers
- **Scalable Growth**: Identifying sustainable, compound growth tactics
- **Learning Velocity**: Fast feedback loops and knowledge accumulation
- **Resource Efficiency**: Doing more with less through smart prioritization

## When to Use

Use growth experimentation when you need to:
- **Accelerate Growth**: Move beyond linear, predictable growth
- **Find Product-Market Fit**: Validate assumptions and iterate quickly
- **Scale Efficiently**: Identify highest-leverage growth activities
- **Break Through Plateaus**: Discover new growth channels and tactics
- **Optimize Full Funnel**: Improve metrics across entire customer journey
- **Build Growth Culture**: Systematic experimentation mindset
- **Compete Effectively**: Out-iterate and out-learn competitors

### 2. ICE Prioritization Framework

**ICE Scoring System**

```
IMPACT (1-10): How much will this move the North Star Metric?

Score 10:
- Could 10x a key metric
- Addresses major bottleneck
- Affects majority of users
- Example: Core activation feature

Score 5:
- Moderate improvement (20-50%)
- Affects segment of users
- Example: Email campaign optimization

Score 1:
- Minimal impact (<5%)
- Affects few users
- Example: Button color change

Questions to Ask:
□ How many users will this affect?
□ What's the realistic impact on our NSM?
□ Is this addressing a major pain point?
□ Does data support high impact?

CONFIDENCE (1-10): How confident are we this will work?

Score 10:
- Backed by strong data
- Proven in similar companies
- Customer requests
- Obvious broken experience

Score 5:
- Some supporting evidence
- Logical hypothesis
- Mixed signals

Score 1:
- Pure speculation
- No supporting data
- Contradicts research
- "Wouldn't it be cool if..."

Questions to Ask:
□ What evidence supports this?
□ Have similar companies done this?
□ What does user research say?
□ Are we fixing a known problem?

EASE (1-10): How easy is it to run this experiment?

Score 10:
- No code required
- Can launch in hours
- Example: Email copy change

Score 5:
- Moderate development
- 1-2 week timeline
- Example: New landing page

Score 1:
- Significant engineering
- Months of work
- Example: Platform rebuild

Questions to Ask:
□ What resources are needed?
□ How long to implement?
□ What's the risk/complexity?
□ Can we test with minimal build?

ICE SCORE CALCULATION:

Method 1 (Average):
ICE = (Impact + Confidence + Ease) / 3

Experiment A: (8 + 7 + 9) / 3 = 8.0
Experiment B: (10 + 3 + 8) / 3 = 7.0
Winner: Experiment A

Method 2 (Multiplicative):
ICE = (Impact × Confidence) / Effort
Note: Effort = 11 - Ease (invert scale)

Experiment A: (8 × 7) / 2 = 28
Experiment B: (10 × 3) / 3 = 10
Winner: Experiment A

EXAMPLE ICE SCORING:

Idea: Add one-click signup with Google OAuth

Impact: 7
- Could improve activation by 30-50%
- Reduces friction significantly
- Affects all new users

Confidence: 8
- Proven to increase signups
- Data shows 60% abandon at signup
- User feedback: "too many fields"

Ease: 6
- 1 week dev work
- Google OAuth integration
- Some QA needed

ICE Score: (7 + 8 + 6) / 3 = 7.0 (HIGH PRIORITY)

---

Idea: Rebuild entire UI with new design system

Impact: 9
- Could significantly improve engagement
- Modern UI expected by users
- Affects all users

Confidence: 4
- No data supporting this specific need
- Current UI not primary complaint
- Speculative benefit

Ease: 1
- 3-6 months of work
- Entire team involved
- High risk of bugs

ICE Score: (9 + 4 + 1) / 3 = 4.7 (LOW PRIORITY)
```

### 3. Growth Experiment Types

**Acquisition Experiments**

```
CHANNEL EXPERIMENTS:

New Channel Testing:
Hypothesis: "If we test TikTok organic content, we'll acquire 1,000+ users/month at <$10 CAC"

Experiment:
- Create 20 TikTok videos over 2 weeks
- Track views, clicks, signups, CAC
- Measure: Cost per signup vs target ($10)
- Decision: Scale if <$10, iterate if $10-20, kill if >$20

Content Format Testing:
Hypothesis: "Video tutorials will drive 3x more qualified traffic than text guides"

Experiment:
- Create 5 video tutorials
- Create 5 equivalent text guides
- Track: Traffic, time on site, conversion rate
- Compare: Engagement and conversion quality

Partnership Experiments:
Hypothesis: "Integration with [Tool X] will drive 500+ signups/month"

Experiment:
- Build integration (MVP)
- Launch in their marketplace
- Track: Views, installs, activations
- Measure: CAC vs other channels

PAID ACQUISITION:

Ad Creative Testing:
- Variation 1: Pain-point focused
- Variation 2: Benefit-focused
- Variation 3: Social proof
- Variation 4: Founder story
→ Measure: CTR, CPA, LTV

Audience Testing:
- Lookalike audiences
- Interest-based targeting
- Behavioral targeting
- Retargeting segments
→ Measure: Quality score, conversion rate

Landing Page Matching:
- Ad variant A → Landing page A
- Ad variant B → Landing page B
→ Measure: Message match impact on conversion
```

**Activation Experiments**

```
ONBOARDING EXPERIMENTS:

Onboarding Flow:
Hypothesis: "Reducing onboarding from 5 steps to 2 will increase activation by 40%"

Experiment Setup:
Control: 5-step onboarding (profile, preferences, invite team, tutorial, first action)
Variant: 2-step onboarding (profile, first action) with optional steps later

Metrics:
- Step completion rate
- Time to first value
- Day 1 activation rate
- Day 7 retention

Empty State Optimization:
Hypothesis: "Interactive tutorial in empty state will increase feature adoption by 50%"

Control: Static "Get started" message
Variant: Interactive walkthrough with sample data

Metrics:
- Feature adoption rate
- Time to first use
- User engagement

Time-to-Value:
Hypothesis: "Showing value within 60 seconds will increase retention by 30%"

Experiments:
- Pre-populate with demo data
- Template gallery
- AI-assisted setup
- One-click quick start

Metrics:
- Time to first "aha moment"
- Activation rate
- D1/D7/D30 retention

AHA MOMENT IDENTIFICATION:

Methodology:
1. Identify activated users (retained for 30+ days)
2. Analyze their first-session behavior
3. Find common actions/patterns
4. Test if forcing these actions increases retention

Example (Slack):
Aha Moment: Send 2,000 messages (team)
Experiment: Get teams to 2,000 messages faster
- Improve onboarding
- Encourage team invites
- Promote key features
- Reduce friction
```

**Retention Experiments**

```
ENGAGEMENT EXPERIMENTS:

Email Re-engagement:
Hypothesis: "Personalized win-back emails will reactivate 15% of churned users"

Experiment:
Control: Generic "We miss you" email
Variant 1: Personalized based on past usage
Variant 2: Special offer (50% off)
Variant 3: Feature update announcement

Metrics:
- Reactivation rate
- Engagement after return
- Long-term retention

Feature Discovery:
Hypothesis: "In-app tooltips will increase feature adoption by 35%"

Control: No tooltips
Variant: Contextual tooltips for underused features

Metrics:
- Feature adoption rate
- User engagement
- Feature stickiness

Notification Strategy:
Hypothesis: "Weekly digest emails will increase D7 retention by 20%"

Experiment:
- Test frequency (daily vs weekly vs monthly)
- Test content (updates vs tips vs user activity)
- Test timing (morning vs evening)
- Test personalization level

Metrics:
- Open rate
- Click rate
- Return rate
- Unsubscribe rate

HABIT FORMATION:

21-Day Activation Challenge:
Hypothesis: "Daily streak gamification will increase D21 retention by 50%"

Mechanics:
- Track daily usage
- Show streak count
- Reward milestones
- Send reminder if streak at risk

Metrics:
- Daily active users (DAU)
- 21-day retention
- Feature usage frequency
```

**Revenue Experiments**

```
PRICING EXPERIMENTS:

Price Point Testing:
Hypothesis: "Increasing price from $49 to $79 will increase revenue 40% with <20% drop in conversions"

Experiment:
- Control: $49/month
- Variant: $79/month
- Measure: Conversion rate, revenue per visitor, customer quality

Packaging Changes:
Hypothesis: "3-tier pricing will increase AOV by 25% vs 2-tier"

Control: Starter ($29), Pro ($99)
Variant: Starter ($29), Pro ($79), Enterprise ($199)

Metrics:
- Plan distribution
- Average revenue per user (ARPU)
- Upgrade rate

Free Trial Length:
Hypothesis: "30-day trial will convert 20% better than 14-day"

Control: 14-day free trial
Variant 1: 30-day free trial
Variant 2: No trial, freemium model

Metrics:
- Trial signup rate
- Trial-to-paid conversion
- Quality of conversions (LTV)

UPSELL & EXPANSION:

In-App Upgrade Prompts:
Hypothesis: "Usage-based upgrade prompts will increase expansion revenue 35%"

Trigger: User hits plan limit
Control: Generic upgrade message
Variant: Personalized message showing value unlocked

Metrics:
- Upgrade conversion rate
- Expansion MRR
- Churn after upgrade

Feature Gating:
Hypothesis: "Gating advanced analytics will drive 25% more Pro upgrades"

Experiment:
- Show locked features with value preview
- One-click upgrade
- Show savings vs add-on pricing

Metrics:
- Feature interest (clicks on locked features)
- Upgrade rate
- Feature adoption post-upgrade
```

**Referral Experiments**

```
VIRAL LOOP EXPERIMENTS:

Referral Program Design:
Hypothesis: "Two-sided incentive ($20 for both) will 3x referrals vs one-sided"

Control: $20 for referrer only
Variant 1: $20 for both referrer and referee
Variant 2: $10 for both
Variant 3: 1 month free for both

Metrics:
- Referral rate (% of users who refer)
- Conversion rate (% of referrals who sign up)
- K-factor (viral coefficient)
- CAC reduction

Viral Mechanics:
Hypothesis: "Built-in collaboration features will increase viral coefficient by 50%"

Experiments:
- Shareable boards/documents
- Team features requiring invites
- Public profiles
- Social sharing incentives

Metrics:
- Invites sent per user
- Invite acceptance rate
- K-factor = (Invites sent × Accept rate × Activation rate)

Timing of Ask:
Hypothesis: "Asking for referral after 'aha moment' will increase participation 3x"

Control: Ask immediately after signup
Variant 1: Ask after first success/value
Variant 2: Ask after 7 days of usage
Variant 3: Ask never, make referral earn rewards

Metrics:
- Referral program participation
- Quality of referrals
- Long-term impact on K-factor
```

## Best Practices

### Growth Experiment Principles

```
✓ Bias toward action (test > debate)
✓ Focus on learning velocity (not just wins)
✓ Start with high-impact, high-confidence
✓ Build experiment muscle with quick wins
✓ Document everything (learning compounds)
✓ Celebrate failures (killed bad ideas fast)
✓ Think in bets (probabilistic thinking)
✓ Cross-functional collaboration
✓ Long-term thinking (sustainable growth)
✓ Ethical growth (no growth hacks that harm users)
```

### Common Growth Mistakes

```
❌ AVOID:

- "Let's try everything and see what works"
  → Scattered efforts, no learning

- Testing without research
  → Random walks, not progress

- Focusing only on acquisition
  → Leaky bucket problem

- Ignoring retention
  → Expensive, unsustainable growth

- Chasing vanity metrics
  → Growth that doesn't drive business value

- Gaming metrics
  → Short-term wins, long-term damage

- Not documenting learnings
  → Repeating mistakes, losing knowledge

- Analysis paralysis
  → Overthinking, under-executing

- Declaring victory too early
  → False positives, wasted resources

- Optimizing before product-market fit
  → Polishing something people don't want
```
